Where the facts come from

Angles, radii, perimeters and areas use the standard formulas for regular polygons and star polygons {n/d}. A star's “area inside the outline” is the area of the region it encloses. Its “area counted by winding” counts the middle again for each extra time the outline goes round, which is what the shoelace formula gives when you trace the star.

The number of points where the diagonals of a regular polygon cross uses Bjorn Poonen and Michael Rubinstein's 1998 formula. It was checked here against a direct count for every polygon up to 120 sides, and it counts a point where three or more diagonals meet only once. The number of regions the diagonals make was counted directly, so it is given up to 120 sides.

A regular polygon can be drawn with compass and straightedge exactly when its number of sides is a power of 2 times distinct Fermat primes (3, 5, 17, 257, 65537), the Gauss–Wantzel theorem. The number of ways to cut a polygon into triangles with diagonals that don't cross is a Catalan number.
