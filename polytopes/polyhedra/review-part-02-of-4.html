Names, alternative names and index numbers come from the open-source Antiprism project (MIT licence): U is the uniform polyhedron number (Maeder's index), C Coxeter's, W Wenninger's model number, and K the Kaleido program's. The short names such as “sirsid” are Jonathan Bowers'. George Olshevsky's names for the nonconvex snub polyhedra are also listed in Antiprism, and in a Wikipedia reference desk discussion from 3 May 2012. Only “yog-sothoth” is widely used.

Some sources disagree with each other. Wolfram's data marks the great snub dodecicosidodecahedron (U64) as not chiral, but its own model has no mirror symmetry, and Wikipedia gives it rotation-only symmetry. This page says chiral.
